What changed compared to MailFlow

Plimail is a fork of MailFlow, the self-hosted unified webmail by Matt. It keeps MailFlow's mail engine and adds what we were missing, then removes what we did not need, so the whole thing is one image to run.

Why a fork

MailFlow lacked features and responsiveness we needed: no calendar, no encryption, and a crowded interface with many small buttons. Plimail was started to add those, on a stack small enough for one person to run and keep up to date.

The rule for Plimail is keep what is necessary, and keep the stack simple: one application image, a 60-line compose file, a 27-line .env.example, and no HTTPS machinery of its own beyond a self-signed fallback. A trusted certificate comes from the reverse proxy or tunnel you already run.

Plimail still takes fixes from MailFlow. The first release ported upstream's 12/24-hour clock, menus kept on screen, the collapsible attachment list, the undo-send switch, per-account notification mute and three IMAP sync fixes.

What was removed, and why

Each removal is a part we did not use and did not want to maintain. If you rely on one of them, MailFlow is the better choice.

Plugins and the GTD plugin

A platform to maintain for features we did not use. Plimail's additions went into the core instead.

Todoist and CardDAV contact sync

Integrations with outside services. The CardDAV server for your own contacts stays.

Built-in antispam and mailbox cleanup

Spam is filtered by the mail server. Sieve rules now run there too.

Electron and Android apps

On a computer, the installable web app does the job of the Electron one with no separate release.

Bundled Caddy and nginx

One image instead of two. Let's Encrypt is left to your own proxy or tunnel.

22 extra themes

One design in dark and light, matching the selfhostr family.

Thanks to MailFlow

Plimail would not exist without MailFlow and the work Matt and its contributors put into it: the IMAP engine, the sync model, the sanitizer and most of the interface come from there. Copyright in that code stays with its authors.

MailFlow is licensed under the GNU AGPL-3.0, and so is Plimail. The original notices are kept, and every change is listed in the NOTICE file. Internal names that installs depend on, such as MAILFLOW_* variables, the mailflow database and the volume names, are unchanged. MailFlow's commercial licence offer does not apply to Plimail.
